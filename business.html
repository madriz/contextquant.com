Business applications

A short list of names that deserve a closer look, and why

ContextQuant does not replace your analysts, your rating models or your terminals. It reads what they do not have time to read, every quarter, and tells them which companies' own words have turned worse, with the passages behind each flag.

Credit risk

Surveillance of the names the agency still calls stable

The problem

Rating agencies move slowly, and a portfolio has more issuers than a team can re-read every quarter. Most deterioration is visible in disclosures before it shows up in a rating, but nobody reads all of them.

What ContextQuant gives

A quarterly ranked watch list of issuers whose filings read worse than their last one, with the counted disclosures (liquidity and refinancing language, new risk factors, guidance cuts, management-attributed headwinds) and the quoted passages behind them.

The evidence

Flagged tenth downgraded within a year: 23.4% against 7.2% (3.3x), a third of all downgrades. Among issuers the agency still rated stable: 16.9% against 4.2% (4.1x). Before downgrades, 68% of downgraded companies were flagged against 28% of matched companies at the same rating. The reading keeps its weight beside distance to default, Altman Z, Ohlson O and 23 volatility measures.

Workflow

Each quarter: flags arrive after the filing season; analysts review the flagged names that the agency still rates stable first; each flag links to its passages; decisions and outcomes are logged.

Equity research

Triage at the scale of the market

The problem

A research team reads 20 to 40 filings a quarter per analyst. A third of the companies in a broad universe are followed by fewer than 12 analysts. The filing that changed most is often the one nobody read.

What ContextQuant gives

A peer-relative score for every filing the day it is filed: how much worse (or better) it reads than the company's last one and than its competitors, and which passages moved.

The evidence

The reading identifies the companies whose disclosures deteriorated most against their own last filing and their peers, and that deterioration is followed by credit downgrades far more often than chance. It is built to decide what to read first and where risk is building, not as a trading signal.

Workflow

Morning list of new filings ranked by change against peers; drill into the flagged passages; add to coverage notes.

Wealth management

A credit early-warning light beside each holding

The problem

Advisors hold hundreds of issuers across client portfolios, in bonds and equities, and need to know which ones deserve a conversation before a downgrade forces one.

What ContextQuant gives

A flag on each holding whose latest filing turned worse, with a plain-language reason drawn from the company's own words, suitable for suitability reviews and client conversations.

The evidence

About a quarter of flagged companies were downgraded within a year, against one in fourteen overall. A flag is a reason to look, not a sell signal: most flagged companies were not downgraded.

Workflow

Upload or sync holdings; see flagged positions and reasons; schedule reviews; track outcomes.

If you buy sentiment data

Standard sentiment adds nothing to a credit model

Word-count sentiment and FinBERT tone from company filings, as levels or quarterly changes, raw or measured against peers, add no information to a downgrade model that already holds the rating, outlook and market data. The ContextQuant reading does. (Scope: company filings and credit outcomes; news and social-media sentiment were not tested.)
