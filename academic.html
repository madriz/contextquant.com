Academic program

A pre-registered test of language-model filing analysis

ContextQuant tests what corporate disclosures, read by a dictionary, by FinBERT and by a large language model, predict about credit risk, under rules fixed in advance, with each test run once and direct tests for model memory. Collaboration, co-authorship and data-access inquiries are welcome.

Design

How the program is run

Hypotheses, outcomes, thresholds and expected signs are registered and git-tagged before testing; extensions are dated amendments. Methods were designed on 2015 to 2020; every reported result is out of sample (2021 to mid-2026). Inference uses 2,000 company-clustered bootstrap resamples, Benjamini-Hochberg correction within families, permutation tests, Fama-MacBeth regressions with Newey-West errors, and out-of-sample AUC.

Credit models control for rating level, history and outlook, returns, volatility, size, leverage, coverage, the last surprise, market, sector and 13 macro series, then for Merton distance to default, Altman Z, Ohlson O, 23 volatility measures and the volatility of fundamentals, then for Lazy Prices and Brown-Tucker text-change measures.

Memory

Testing for look-ahead in language models

The model's training data post-date the outcomes. The program blanks identities; asks the model, with no document, what happened to each company and uses its answer as a control; tests whether signals are stronger where it claims more knowledge; measures run-to-run noise; and locks scores on post-cutoff filings with SHA-256 fingerprints before outcomes exist. The credit predictions kept 90% to 96% of their strength under these tests.
