Methodology

Rules first, results second

Most claims about text signals come from back-tests tuned until they look good. ContextQuant works the other way round: every test is written down and time-stamped before the data is examined, and runs once.

What is read

Public text, at scale

Every quarterly and annual report (10-Q, 10-K, 20-F, 40-F) and, where available, every earnings-call transcript of 886 US-listed companies from 2015 to 2026: the Dow 30 and Nasdaq-100 with their competitors, plus banks, insurers, healthcare and semiconductor companies. 129,974 filings, 16,035 calls, about a billion words, all from public sources.

Each reading is compared with the company's own industry and its named competitors in the same quarter, so a company is judged against the right yardstick: banks always sound cautious; software companies always sound upbeat.

The readings

Three ways to read, compared head to head

A finance word list (the academic standard), FinBERT (a small language model trained on financial text) and a large language model reading the filing the way an analyst would, including against the company's previous filing and its numbers.

A measurement reading is also used: the model lists credit-relevant disclosures with exact quotes, every quote is checked against the filing, and a fixed formula turns them into an index. Memory cannot add a quote that is not there. Prompts, fields and formulas are proprietary.

The checks

What every result has to survive

CheckWhat it means
Pre-registrationHypotheses, outcomes, thresholds and expected directions written down and time-stamped in version control before any test. Each extension is a numbered, dated amendment.
One runEach registered test runs once. No re-runs with different settings, no picking the best version.
Out of sampleMethods designed on 2015 to 2020; every reported result is from 2021 to 2026.
Company-clustered intervals2,000 re-draws of whole companies, so a few companies with many filings cannot dominate.
Correction for many testsBenjamini-Hochberg within every family of tests, so false discoveries stay rare.
Permutation testsReadings shuffled at random within each quarter; shuffled readings almost never match the real ones.
Full controlsCredit results are judged after rating, outlook, market data, distance to default, Altman Z, Ohlson O, 23 volatility measures and 13 economic series.
Timing auditEvery outcome checked to start after the filing was public, so a reading is never credited with agreeing with news already out.
Memory testsCompany names hidden; the model asked directly what it remembers and that answer held fixed; and filings written after its training ended scored and locked before their outcomes exist.
Why memory matters

A model can remember the future of the past

A language model trained on text up to mid-2026 may have read what happened to a company in 2023. When it reads that company's 2022 filing, its "prediction" could be recall. In production this cannot help, because every forecast is about a future that has not happened. In a back-test it can inflate results.

That is why every reported result is tested for it. The credit results kept 90% to 96% of their strength under every memory test, and the final check runs on filings written after the model's training ended.

Models change

Built to outlive a model version

Language models are retired. The measurement reading is designed so that a new model can be checked against the old one item by item: the same quotes should be found, and the formula and history carry over. Each new model version gets its own time-stamped post-cutoff record.
