2. Beyond the standard toolkit

It adds to what a credit analyst already uses

Each reading was added to a downgrade model that already contains the rating, rating history and outlook, stock return and volatility, size, leverage, analyst coverage, the last earnings surprise, market and sector, 13 economic series, distance to default, Altman Z, Ohlson O, 23 volatility measures (including GARCH and Yang-Zhang) and the volatility of earnings, sales and cash flow.

A step is one standard deviation of the reading. In one model with every rival at once, only the Change Signal and the Credit Stress Index keep significant weight. Honest limit: the agencies' own information already ranks companies well (accuracy 0.875), so no text input lifts the overall ranking much. The value is in the tail: the flagged names, and companies the agency still calls stable.

7. How to read these results

Where the readings add most

Beside a credit model, as a watch list. A model with the rating, outlook and market data already ranks companies well (accuracy 0.77 to 0.88). Adding the readings concentrates downgrades in the flagged names but did not lift the 2024 test-year accuracy after correction, even among calm companies.

Where the agency is still calm. When S&P had already put a company on negative outlook or watch, the flag came first in 25% to 48% of those downgrades. The value is at companies the agency still calls stable.

The final memory check is locked and pending. The language model was trained on text up to mid-2026, so it may know what happened to these companies. Asking it directly what it remembers, and holding that fixed, removes only 4% to 10% of the credit results, and they work as well on companies it says it knows little about. The clean test is on filings written after its training ended: see the track record.
