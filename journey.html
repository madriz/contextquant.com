The journey

Where it started, where it is, where it can go

ContextQuant began with promising signals in three sector studies. Scaling the research and testing it to a higher standard exposed gaps in the first methodology. They were closed with better tools, and what came through is sharper and more useful: an early warning of credit trouble, written in the companies' own words.

Where it started

Spring 2026. Three studies, 222 companies, 32,983 filings. Filing language carried measurable information about what came next.

Where it is

November 2026. 886 companies, 129,974 filings, 1.03 billion words, about 8,700 pre-registered tests. A tested credit early warning.

Where it can go

A quarterly locked record, more markets and sectors, credit-market outcomes, and alternative data that moves before published results.

1. Where it started

Three studies and an encouraging first answer

The first program studied 37 banks and insurers, 34 healthcare companies and a 185-company diversified universe from 2015 to 2025. It read the management discussion and risk factors of every filing with a finance word list and a large language model, and compared each company with its peers.

The results were encouraging: filing language carried measurable information about later outcomes, and the language model read something the word list could not. That was enough to justify a much larger and much stricter study.

2. What was strengthened

Higher standards exposed gaps, and they were closed

At scale, three questions mattered more than any single result. Was every outcome measured strictly after the filing was public? Could the language model simply remember what happened next? Did the readings add anything to the tools a professional already uses?

Answering them changed the method, and the method changed the focus: from a broad set of signals to the one that holds under every test, credit early warning.

What was added to the method

October and November 2026

SCALE

Five universes, 886 companies

The Dow 30 and Nasdaq-100 with their competitors, plus semiconductors, banks and insurers, and healthcare: 129,974 filings, 60,815 reports read in full, 16,035 earnings calls, 21,230 executive-change filings, 1.03 billion words.

PRE-REGISTRATION

Rules first, results second

Every hypothesis, outcome and threshold written down and time-stamped before testing; each test run once; extensions added as dated amendments (nine so far).

TIMING

Every outcome strictly after the filing

Each outcome checked to begin after the filing was public, so a signal is never credited with agreeing with news already out.

THE PROFESSIONAL'S TOOLKIT

Controls a credit analyst would use

Rating, history and outlook; market data; distance to default, Altman Z and Ohlson O; 23 volatility measures; the volatility of earnings, sales and cash flow; 13 macro series; and published text-change measures as rivals.

MEMORY

Making sure the AI reads rather than remembers

Company identities hidden; the model asked directly what it remembers and that answer held fixed; results compared where it knows more and less; repeated readings averaged; and filings written after its training ended scored and locked before outcomes exist.

MEASUREMENT

A reading every flag can be checked against

A credit stress index built from disclosures listed with exact quotes, each verified against the filing, combined by a fixed formula.

FAIR BASELINES

Compared with the right yardstick

Downgraded companies compared with matched companies at the same rating over the same window; results measured among companies the rating agency still called stable.

4. Where it can go

Roadmap

A record that builds itself

Every quarter, new filings scored and locked with a SHA-256 fingerprint before outcomes exist. First readout March 2027; the credit readout as downgrades accumulate.

More markets and sectors

From five universes to broad US coverage (about 4,000 listed companies), Canadian issuers through SEDAR+, and dedicated sector studies.

Credit-market outcomes

Beyond rating actions: credit-default-swap and bond-spread moves, and covenant and refinancing events.

Signals before the numbers

Alternative data that moves before companies report: product and app ratings, search interest, government contracts, developer activity, clinical trials and recalls. Already mapped company by company for 888 entities, from free public sources.

Delivery

A quarterly watch list, company drill-downs with the passages behind each flag, portfolio checks and alerts. See the product preview.

Partners

Pilots with credit, research and wealth teams, with success criteria written down before they start. Partnership.
